Specimen catalog

Six species, chosen for how differently they've solved the same problem: staying alive with no bones and no backup.

Not all spiders build webs. Roughly half of known species hunt without one — running down prey, ambushing from burrows, or stalking in daylight with eyesight sharper than their size suggests. The catalog below mixes web-builders and wanderers on purpose.

warm forest

Golden Silk Orb-Weaver

Trichonephila clavipes

Spins webs with a visible golden cast in low light. Females can be twenty times the mass of males, and the web itself is often rebuilt overnight, thread by thread.

grassland

Wolf Spider

Lycosidae

Hunts on foot instead of waiting in silk. Mothers carry their egg sac attached to the spinnerets, then let the spiderlings ride on their back until the first molt.

open scrub

Peacock Jumping Spider

Maratus volans

Males raise iridescent abdominal flaps and drum out a rhythm to court a female — one of the few courtship displays built as much for sound as for color.

bark & crevice

Huntsman Spider

Sparassidae

Flattened enough to slide under loose bark, and able to shift legs into a crab-like sideways gait — one of the fastest walkers, relative to size, of any land animal.

dry shelter

Redback Spider

Latrodectus hasseltii

A close relative of the black widow, marked by a red stripe down the abdomen. The bite is medically significant but rarely dangerous now that antivenom is widely available.

arid burrow

Chilean Rose Tarantula

Grammostola rosea

Slow-moving and famously unbothered by handling, which has made it one of the most kept tarantulas. Females can live more than twenty years — decades longer than most spiders.
